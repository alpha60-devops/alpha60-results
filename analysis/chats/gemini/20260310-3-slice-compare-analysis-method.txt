### 1. Overview and Patterns Observed
Both pages display data collection sets from **Alpha60**, aiming to track the peer-to-peer (BitTorrent) file-sharing swarms of specific film and television media spanning from 2019 to 2026. 

**Key Patterns:**
* **Intersectionality & Overlap:** Two titles appear in both datasets: *Mr. and Mrs. Smith (2024)* (which stars Donald Glover and Maya Erskine) and *Summer of Soul*. This highlights that "led" categories are not mutually exclusive and that modern media frequently features intersectional casts/creators.
* **Formatting and Structure:** Both sets follow a strict naming convention, treating feature films as base titles (e.g., *everything-everywhere-all-at-once*, *american-fiction*) and television series as granular, episode-specific items coded by season and episode (e.g., `shogun-2024-101`, `atlanta-301`). 
* **Franchise and Genre Heaviness:** Both lists show a heavy reliance on major sci-fi/fantasy franchises and comic book adaptations (e.g., *Eternals*, *Shang-Chi*, *Ahsoka*, *The Acolyte*, *Black Panther*, *Falcon and the Winter Soldier*, *Gen V*). 
* **Streaming Platform Exclusivity:** The vast majority of the media tracked across both lists (e.g., Netflix's *Squid Game*, Amazon's *Underground Railroad*, Disney+'s *American Born Chinese*) are exclusive to specific subscription streaming services. 

### 2. Suggested Conclusions
* **Piracy as a Proxy for Global Demand:** Because these lists track BitTorrent sharing, the presence of these diverse, minority-led titles in large swarms likely indicates strong global demand for diverse media. Often, global audiences resort to piracy when streaming platforms hosting these shows are unavailable or cost-prohibitive in their regions.
* **"Tentpole" Franchises Drive Visibility:** The heavy presence of Marvel, Star Wars, and major sci-fi IPs suggests that diverse casting within existing massive franchises generates some of the highest peer-to-peer sharing activity, potentially driving more global visibility than independent films.
* **Categorical Conflation:** In the AAPI list, there seems to be a conflation between **Asian-American** media (e.g., *American Born Chinese*, *Interior Chinatown*, *The Brothers Sun*) and **International Asian** media (e.g., *Squid Game*, *All of Us Are Dead*, *Space Sweepers*). Calling South Korean productions "AAPI" (Asian American and Pacific Islander) is a misnomer, as they are native Korean productions, not American.

### 3. Methodological Improvements
To improve the rigor, accuracy, and utility of this data, the following methodological adjustments should be considered:

* **Refine the Taxonomy (Distinguish Diasporic vs. Native Media):** The project should separate "AAPI" (a specifically American demographic term) from "Asian" or "Global South." Tracking K-Dramas or Chinese blockbusters in the same bucket as Asian-American diaspora narratives muddles the cultural analysis.
* **Establish a Control Group:** To understand if "Black-Led" or "AAPI-Led" media behaves differently in BitTorrent swarms, the study needs a comparative baseline. Analyzing the swarms of similarly budgeted, non-minority-led media (e.g., comparing *The Acolyte* to *Andor*, or *Mr. and Mrs. Smith* to *Reacher*) would provide necessary context on whether the piracy rates are influenced by the demographic of the cast or simply by the genre and marketing budget.
* **Account for VPN Obfuscation:** The datasets generate `GeoJSON` files mapping the IP addresses of the swarms. However, because piracy inherently involves VPN usage, geographic location data pulled from IP addresses is highly likely to be skewed toward popular VPN server locations (like Switzerland, the Netherlands, or specific US cities). The methodology should note this limitation or attempt to filter out known VPN IP blocks.
* **Formalize Intersectional Tagging:** Instead of duplicating entries like *Mr. and Mrs. Smith* across isolated lists, the database could use a single, unified media list with relational tagging (e.g., `Tags: [AAPI-led, Black-led, Female-led]`). This would prevent data redundancy and make cross-sectional data analysis much easier. 
* **Standardize TV Sampling:** For television shows, the sampling is somewhat erratic. Some shows only track the pilot episode (`101`), while others track a random later episode (like `devs-108` or `euphoria-208`). Establishing a consistent rule (e.g., "always track the premiere and finale of a season") would make comparative metrics much more reliable.
